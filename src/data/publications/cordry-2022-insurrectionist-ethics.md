    heading: "§1 · Beyond Polite Reformism in American Philosophy"
    body: "How should ethical theory respond when legal and deliberative institutions are themselves engines of racialized and economic subjugation? The contributors to Carter and Scriven's volume demonstrate that insurrectionist ethics—centered on figures such as David Walker, Maria Stewart, John Brown, and Leonard Harris—offers an indispensable corrective to consensus-bound deliberative frameworks [1]."
  - id: "sec-praxis"
    heading: "§2 · Character, Advocacy & Liberatory Praxis"
    body: "By evaluating the epistemic and moral virtues required for insurgent resistance—audacity, tenacity, and solidarity with the subjugated—the volume bridges American pragmatism, Africana philosophy, and social-political critique. Rather than asking how oppressed populations might wait for moral persuasion to sway their oppressors, insurrectionist ethics asks what duties arise immediately in the face of systemic atrocity [2]."
  - id: "sec-orientation"
    heading: "§3 · Spiritual Fortitude & Collective Re-Orientation"
