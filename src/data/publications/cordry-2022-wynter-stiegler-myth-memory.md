abstract: "Recipient of the Caribbean Philosophical Association's Claudia Jones Award, this paper stages a critical encounter between Sylvia Wynter's anticolonial poetics of the human ('homo narrans' and the sociogenic principle) and Bernard Stiegler's philosophy of technics ('epiphylogenesis' and tertiary retention). Whereas Stiegler demonstrates how technical artifacts exteriorize and condition human memory, Wynter reveals how mythic origin narratives and colonial genres of 'Man' biosymbolically program collective consciousness. Reading Wynter and Stiegler together exposes both the colonial blind spots of Eurocentric media theory and the technical materiality underlying sociogenic codes."

  - id: "sec-sociogeny"
    heading: "§1 · Homo Narrans & The Sociogenic Principle"
    body: "Building on Frantz Fanon's insight that beside phylogeny and ontogeny stands sociogeny [1], Sylvia Wynter argues that human beings are hybrid creatures—simultaneously biological organisms (bios) and storytelling beings (mythoi). Every social order institutes a descriptive statement of the human that neurochemically and affectively orients collective life."
  - id: "sec-epiphylogenesis"
    heading: "§2 · Epiphylogenesis & Technical Exteriorization"
    body: "Bernard Stiegler's reading of André Leroi-Gourhan situates the origin of the human in the co-evolution of cortex and flint tool—a process of epiphylogenesis whereby memory is inscribed in inorganic matter outside the biological genome [2]. Yet Stiegler's account of grammatization largely neglects how racial capitalism and colonial modernity bifurcated technical systems along the color line."
  - id: "sec-encounter"
    heading: "§3 · Decolonial Pharmacology of Myth and Memory"
    body: "Synthesizing Wynter's third-event science of the word with Stiegler's pharmacology of tertiary retention allows us to understand how technical infrastructures stabilize or subvert hegemonic genres of Man, opening a path toward decolonial practices of technical and mythic re-orientation."
